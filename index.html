What is Leduc Hold'em?

Leduc Hold'em is a simplified 2-player poker game designed for studying game theory and GTO strategy. It uses a tiny deck and two short betting rounds, making it small enough for humans to learn GTO-optimal strategy, but complex enough to capture all key poker concepts: pot odds, bluffs, ranges as well as checking, betting and calling frequencies. It is ideal for understanding all poker concepts and developing general game theory intuition.

The deck

6 cards total: Jack (J), Queen (Q), King (K), two suits. K beats Q and Q beats Jack.

How a hand plays

  1. Both players post a blind of 1 chip.
  2. Each player is dealt one private card face-down.
  3. Round 1 — first betting round. Bet size is 2, raise is to 4.
  4. A public card is dealt face-up (the board).
  5. Round 2 — second betting round. Bet size is 4, raise is to 8.
  6. If both players are still in, there is a showdown.

Showdown

A pair (your private card matches the board card) beats any non-paired hand regardless of rank. If neither player has a pair, the higher card wins: K > Q > J. Identical holdings split the pot.

Betting round

When no bet or raise has been made yet, a player can either keep things as is (check) or put more money in the pot (bet). Facing a bet, the opponent must either match it (call), exceed it (raise), or surrender the pot (fold). When facing a raise, the only options are to call or to fold — only one raise per round is allowed. The hand ends if a player folds; otherwise it continues to the next round or showdown.

Actions

CheckPass the action without betting (only when no bet is open).
BetOpen the betting for this round.
CallMatch the opponent's bet to stay in.
RaiseIncrease the bet by the round amount. Max 1 raise per round.
FoldSurrender your cards and lose your chips invested so far.
